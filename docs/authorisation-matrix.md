# Authorisation matrix

Every function that changes state in Sigvara's contracts: who may call it, what has to hold for the
call to succeed, and what it changes. It covers the five registries and the SVR faucet token as they
are on `main`. `SigvaraReader` only reads, so it has no table.

[`scripts/check-authz.mjs`](../scripts/check-authz.mjs) reads `src/` in CI and fails when:

- a state-changing function has no row, or a row names a function that no longer exists;
- a row's **Caller** no longer matches the function's access modifiers;
- a row says `checked` for a function whose body never looks at the caller;
- a row's **Guard** no longer matches `whenInflowsOpen`;
- another contract in `src/` gains a state-changing function and has no table.

The **Requires** and **Effect** columns are prose. The script does not read them, so they are
reviewed with the change that touches the function, like any other documentation.

## Reading a row

| Caller | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `role:NAME` | `onlyRole(NAME)`: the caller must hold that role on this contract |
| `owner` | `onlyOwner`, on the SVR faucet token only |
| `initializer`, `reinitializer:N` | Runs once per proxy: at deployment, or with an upgrade |
| `checked` | No access modifier. The body decides who may call, for example only the agent's operator, and **Requires** says who |
| `anyone` | Any account may call, within what **Requires** lists. These are the permissionless paths, such as finalizing a score or executing a slash once its window has closed |
| `inherited` | An OpenZeppelin function the contract inherits, described as it applies here |

**Guard** is `inflows` when the guardian's pause closes the function (`whenInflowsOpen`), and `-`
otherwise. A pause lasts three days and ends by itself. It closes new entries only: registrations,
stake and bond deposits, and score proposals. Withdrawals, disputes, slash rulings and execution, and
every admin call stay open.

## Roles and who grants them

A role is granted and revoked by its admin role (`getRoleAdmin`). On `main`, each `initialize` sets
these:

| Role | On | Granted by | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` | the five registries | `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` | The admin Safe on mainnet: parameters, rulings, vetoes, oracle and guardian keys |
| `UPGRADER_ROLE` | the five registries | `UPGRADER_ROLE` | Held by the 48 hour timelock on mainnet, so the admin cannot grant it to itself |
| `STAKING_CORE_ROLE` | identity, reputation | `UPGRADER_ROLE` | It changes an agent's standing |
| `SLASHING_COMMITTEE_ROLE` | reputation, staking | `UPGRADER_ROLE` | It files slashes and rejects scores |
| `SLASHER_ROLE` | oracle bond | `UPGRADER_ROLE` | It takes bonds at once; it can never be granted to `slashBeneficiary` |
| `ORACLE_ROLE` | reputation, epoch fees | `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` | A replacement key may be needed in an incident. On reputation it takes no funds; on epoch fees it moves one fee an epoch from an agent's prepaid balance to `collected`, which leaves only for the reward pool the timelock sets, or the burn |
| `GUARDIAN_ROLE` | identity, reputation, staking, oracle bond | `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` | It can pause inflows and do nothing else |

`TimelockedGrants` adds the reverse path. The admin may revoke a role that `UPGRADER_ROLE` grants at
once, except `UPGRADER_ROLE` itself, so installing a slasher or a committee takes the timelock's delay
and removing one does not. `SigvaraEpochFees` is plain `AccessControlUpgradeable`; it is not
deployed, and is left out of launch ([ADR 0005](adr/0005-governance-and-emergency-powers.md)).

Two kinds of account act without a role:

- an agent's **operator**, recorded per agent in `SigvaraIdentity`, who runs the agent's identity,
  stake and fee balance;
- an **oracle operator**, admitted by the admin in `SigvaraOracleBond`, who posts a bond and may name
  a signing key.

The SVR token on testnet is a faucet: its owner mints, and anyone may draw a capped amount a day.

### On the Arc testnet

The testnet proxies run these implementations, but they were initialised by the 21 September code,
and an upgrade does not rewrite role administrators. On them `DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` grants every role,
`UPGRADER_ROLE` and the slasher, committee and staking-core roles included, so the timelocked grants
above do not constrain the testnet. For a mainnet deployment, `scripts/check-mainnet.sh` reads the
administrators of the staking-core, committee and slasher roles and fails unless each is
`UPGRADER_ROLE`. Who holds each role today is in the
README's [Access Control Summary](../README.md#access-control-summary).
